# womens clothes sale: how to spot the real markdown, stack cashback on top, and keep the discount when you return a size

The frustrating part of shopping a women's clothes sale isn't finding one. Half the internet is permanently on sale. The frustrating part is that a 60% off tag on a $89 dress can still end up costing you $89, because the code didn't apply, the size was wrong, the return ate the saving, and you paid full price for shipping.

There's a second layer most shoppers skip entirely: the commission retailers pay to whoever sent you to their checkout. Cashback platforms hand a slice of that back. On clothing it's one of the better-paying categories — ShopBack's own US rate index put the average headline cashback rate for fashion merchants at 10.8% as of 13 August 2026, versus 5.7% across all 53 US merchants it tracks.

Ninety seconds of setup is the difference between a sale price and an actually cheap order.

## The three prices of the same dress

Every women's clothing order carries three separate prices stacked on top of each other, and they're funded by different people:

1. **The markdown.** The retailer cutting its own margin. End-of-season clearance, mid-season promo, flash sale.
2. **The code.** Also the retailer, usually a specific percentage with a minimum spend and a stack of exclusions buried in the T&Cs.
3. **The cashback and card rewards.** Funded by the retailer's affiliate marketing budget and your bank respectively. Neither of these reduces the checkout total — the money comes back to you afterwards.

Only the third layer is invisible at checkout, which is why people forget it exists. It's also the only layer that applies to a full-price item, a 70% off item, a dress in a broken size run, and the socks you added to hit free shipping.

One catch worth knowing before you plan around it: layers one and three can actively conflict. Using a coupon code that isn't listed on the cashback platform can void the whole cashback on that order. Retailers set those rules, and the platform publishes them per store.

## The stacking habit that decides whether any of this works

The tracking rules are boring and they're the whole game. ShopBack's own help documentation lists the failure modes:

- **Start every purchase from the platform.** Opening the retailer's site directly means no tracked session, no cashback. Every single time, not just the first time.
- **Finish in one session.** Switching devices, browsers or tabs mid-checkout can break the attribution.
- **Turn off ad blockers and strict tracking protection** for the checkout — they can block the affiliate cookie outright.
- **Don't click other coupon or cashback sites after you start.** They'll claim the referral instead.
- **Avoid in-app browsers.** The mini browser inside Instagram or a messaging app frequently drops the cookie. Open the link in your normal browser or the app.
- **Clear your cart first at some stores.** Cotton On, for instance, won't track items that were already sitting in the basket before you clicked through.

The reason this matters more for clothing than for most categories: a good women's clothing sale basket is often a bigger single order than people expect. Returning two of five dresses on a $240 order multiplies every tracking mistake you made.

## What kills cashback on a clothing order

Three things, and two of them are specific to clothes:

**Returns.** Any rejected, cancelled, refunded, exchanged or returned purchase stops being eligible. Partial returns get prorated down as an adjustment. In women's fashion, where ordering two sizes and sending one back is normal behaviour, this is the biggest single risk. Buy the size you actually think you are, not three speculative sizes.

**Gift cards and vouchers.** Anything paid with a gift card, voucher or store credit doesn't earn cashback. Fees, taxes and shipping are excluded from the qualifying amount too.

**Discount codes from outside the platform.** A code from a coupon site, a Facebook ad or a newsletter can carry its own affiliate attribution and override the cashback. Use the codes the platform surfaces, or check the store's terms to see whether outside codes are permitted at all.

If you follow all of it and nothing shows up, wait 48 hours before filing a missing cashback report. Most orders register within that window.

## Timing and patience: the honest part

Cashback on clothing isn't instant money, and pretending otherwise wastes your time.

An order shows as **tracked** within about 48 hours. It then sits as **pending** while the merchant's return window is open — retail typically confirms within 30 to 90 days, and some clothing retailers run longer. Only after it moves to **confirmed** can you withdraw. City Chic's confirmation window is listed at 120 days on its platform page; Cotton On's at 90.

On payouts, the US programme supports PayPal and ACH. The Help Center states you can withdraw at $10 in confirmed cashback, while a FAQ section on the same page still says $5 — worth checking in your own account rather than trusting either number. There's a $300 daily withdrawal cap, which only matters if you've been stacking heavily, and payouts are typically processed within 10 calendar days, occasionally up to 30.

The practical version: treat cashback as a refund that arrives a season later. If you're buying a winter coat in a spring clearance, the cashback lands around the time you'd next think about coats.

## A practical order for your next sale purchase

1. Open the app or extension and search the store you already planned to buy from. Check the rate and read the exclusions for that specific retailer.
2. Empty your cart and close the retailer's app if you had items waiting.
3. Click through from ShopBack, then shop in one continuous session — no switching to another tab to compare prices, no other coupon sites.
4. Use only the codes listed on the cashback store page, or confirm on that page that outside codes are allowed.
5. Buy the size you actually want. Ordering three to return two is the most reliable way to lose the cashback.
6. Watch for the tracked entry within 48 hours. If it doesn't appear, file the missing cashback report with your order number and a screenshot.

That's the entire method. The dress costs what it costs; the difference is whether roughly a tenth of it comes back to you a few weeks later or stays with the retailer's marketing budget.
